# Aperture Labs "Halo" Smart Ring — Launch and Privacy Backlash

## Situation Overview

**Date:** March 2026
**Company:** Aperture Labs, a consumer wearables firm based in Austin, Texas
**Product:** Halo, a $349 smart ring measuring sleep, heart-rate variability, and blood oxygen
**Status:** Launched March 4. Backlash began March 9.

## What Happened

Aperture Labs launched the Halo ring to strong pre-orders — 180,000 units in the first
72 hours. On March 9, a security researcher published a teardown showing that the Halo
companion app transmits raw biometric data to third-party analytics servers by default,
including a partner that sells aggregated health signals to insurance underwriters.

The opt-out exists but is buried three levels deep in settings. The privacy policy
discloses the practice in a single sentence on page 14.

Aperture's initial response was a statement calling the report "misleading." That
statement is now widely quoted as an example of what not to do. Six days later the CEO
reversed course and promised a firmware update making the sharing opt-in.

## Key People

**Dr. Maya Restrepo** — Founder and CEO of Aperture Labs. Former Stanford bioengineering
researcher. Built her public reputation on "your body, your data" messaging, which is now
being quoted back at her. Active on X with 340,000 followers. Defensive under pressure.

**Julian Okonkwo** — Independent security researcher who published the teardown on his
blog and in a 40-post thread. Methodical, avoids hyperbole, publishes his methodology.
Has 95,000 followers, mostly technical. Not seeking press attention but getting it.

**Sandra Beale** — Aperture's VP of Communications. Wrote the "misleading" statement.
Now managing a crisis she partly created. Internally arguing for full disclosure.

**Tom Girard** — Aperture's Chief Product Officer. Reportedly pushed the analytics
partnership to improve unit economics. Has not spoken publicly. Rumored to be leaving.

**Priya Raghunathan** — Senior technology reporter at The Verge. Broke the story into
mainstream coverage. Has interviewed Restrepo twice before and is seen as fair but tough.

**Congressman Alan Feld** — Member of the House Energy and Commerce Committee. Sent a
public letter to Aperture demanding answers within 14 days. Has a track record of using
tech controversies to raise his profile.

**Kelsey Nunn** — Popular fitness YouTuber, 2.1 million subscribers. Was a paid Halo
promoter. Posted a tearful video announcing she was returning her sponsorship fee.
Her audience is largely non-technical and trusts her judgment.

**Dr. Ronald Hsu** — Health-privacy academic at Johns Hopkins. Provides sober expert
commentary to journalists. Argues the real problem is regulatory, not one bad company.

## Organizations Involved

**Aperture Labs** — 400-person company, Series C, $220M raised. Reputation was its
primary asset.

**Meridian Analytics** — The third-party data partner. Declined to comment. Sells
aggregated health risk signals to insurance and pharmaceutical clients.

**Electronic Frontier Foundation** — Published a detailed advocacy post calling for an
FTC investigation. Mobilizing its supporter base.

**The Verge** — Drove mainstream coverage.

**Federal Trade Commission** — Has not confirmed an investigation. Sources suggest staff
are reviewing the case.

**r/QuantifiedSelf** — 800,000-member subreddit. Was the product's early evangelist
community and is now its harshest critic. Highly technical, long memories.

**Wearables Consumer Alliance** — Industry trade group. Quietly worried about spillover
regulation affecting all members.

## Current State of Discussion

Sentiment split roughly three ways. Technical communities focus on the default-on
architecture and consider the opt-out placement deliberate. Mainstream consumers are
mostly confused about what data was shared and whether they should return the device.
A vocal minority argues the outrage is overblown because the data was anonymized.

Return requests are up sharply. Pre-orders have slowed but not collapsed. Competitors
are staying conspicuously silent, wary of inviting scrutiny of their own practices.

Open questions driving conversation: Does the firmware update apply retroactively to
data already collected? Will the FTC act? Does Restrepo survive as CEO? Do the insurance
partnerships continue under a different structure?
