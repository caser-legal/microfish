# Riverton Transit Referendum — Measure 7 Light Rail Expansion

## Situation Overview

**Date:** March 2026
**City:** Riverton, a mid-sized US city of roughly 620,000 people
**Issue:** Measure 7, a ballot referendum on a $2.4 billion light rail expansion
**Vote Date:** June 2026. Campaigning is active now.

## What Happened

Riverton's transit authority proposed an 18-mile light rail extension connecting the
downtown core to the eastern suburbs and the regional airport. Funding comes from a
0.7% sales tax increase over 25 years.

Early polling showed 61% support. That has slipped to 48% after a leaked internal memo
revealed the project's cost estimate had risen 40% since the original proposal, and that
the eastern alignment would require acquiring 34 properties in the Fairhaven district,
a historically Black neighborhood, through eminent domain.

The transit authority did not disclose the revised route publicly before the memo leaked.
Supporters argue the project is still essential. Opponents now include an unusual
coalition of fiscal conservatives and Fairhaven residents who normally vote opposite ways.

## Key People

**Mayor Denise Okafor** — Two-term mayor, staked significant political capital on
Measure 7. Champions it as her legacy project. Now defending both the substance and the
lack of disclosure. Up for reelection in 2027.

**Raymond Tsosie** — Executive Director of the Riverton Transit Authority. Career transit
planner, not a politician. Authored the undisclosed route revision. Poor public
communicator, increasingly the face of the controversy.

**Pastor Gloria Means** — Leads Fairhaven Baptist Church, 40 years in the neighborhood.
Emerged as the most credible voice against the current alignment. Careful to say she
supports transit but opposes this route. Enormous local moral authority.

**Curtis Vandenberg** — President of the Riverton Taxpayers Alliance. Opposed every tax
measure for 15 years. Has found unexpected common cause with Fairhaven residents and is
being careful not to overplay it.

**Alicia Fontaine** — City Council member representing Fairhaven. Caught between her
constituents and her party's pro-transit position. Has not taken a formal stance, which
is drawing criticism from both directions.

**Dev Mahajan** — Founder of Riverton Forward, a young pro-transit advocacy group. Frames
the measure as a climate and equity necessity. Sometimes dismissive of Fairhaven concerns,
which has caused friction with allies.

**Nora Whitfield** — Investigative reporter at the Riverton Gazette. Obtained and
published the leaked memo. Continuing to file public records requests.

**Ben Kaminski** — Owner of three restaurants downtown. Vocal supporter, believes rail
brings foot traffic. Organized a business coalition of about 90 members.

**Dr. Lila Ferreira** — Urban planning professor at Riverton State. Provides neutral
technical analysis to media. Argues the alignment could be revised without killing the
project, a position neither campaign wants to hear.

## Organizations Involved

**Riverton Transit Authority** — The agency behind the proposal. Credibility damaged by
the nondisclosure.

**Fairhaven Neighborhood Association** — Organizing residents facing displacement.
Holding weekly meetings with growing attendance.

**Riverton Taxpayers Alliance** — Anti-tax organization funding opposition advertising.

**Riverton Forward** — Pro-transit grassroots group, largely under 35, strong on social
media, weaker on turnout operations.

**Riverton Gazette** — Local paper of record. Editorial board has not yet endorsed.

**Amalgamated Transit Union Local 441** — Represents transit workers. Strongly supports
the measure for the jobs it creates.

**Riverton Chamber of Commerce** — Endorsed the measure but growing nervous as polling
slips.

**Sierra Club Riverton Chapter** — Supports on climate grounds but is internally divided
over the environmental justice implications of the Fairhaven alignment.

## Current State of Discussion

The debate has fractured the usual political alignments. Progressive voters are split
between climate priorities and displacement concerns. Conservative opposition is unified
on cost but uncomfortable allying with an environmental justice argument.

Social media discussion is dominated by two hashtags, one promoting the measure's climate
benefits and one focused on Fairhaven displacement. Neighborhood forums show far more
nuance than the citywide conversation, with many residents saying they want rail but not
this route.

Turnout is the wild card. June referendums historically draw older, more suburban voters.

Open questions: Can the alignment be revised before June, or is it legally locked? Does
Mayor Okafor survive politically if it fails? Will Fairhaven residents turn out to vote
or disengage entirely? Does the Gazette endorse?
